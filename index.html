613 mitzvos · local-first · .NET + Avalonia desktop

A quiet study desk for the Taryag — not another notes app.

TaryagNotes is organized around the 613 mitzvos themselves. Pick a mitzvah, land in the right primary source, write a ha'arah that stays permanently tied to where it came from, and come back tomorrow exactly where you left off — all stored locally, no account or cloud sync required.

The mental model

One loop, five stops.

Everything in the app funnels through the same cycle. There's no dashboard to configure and no feed to scroll — just a path from "I want to learn this mitzvah" back to "what should I do next."

1

Today

Answers one question: where should I continue? Recent & pinned mitzvos, notes due for review.

→
4

Notes

→
5

Review

A plain spaced-repetition pass over your notes — no streaks, no gamification.

Honest routingMitzvah → source routes are "best-available" hints, not guaranteed exact citations. Sometimes it's the exact segment; sometimes just the right neighborhood — the Reader's own navigation and search take it from there.
Shared locationCommentaries (Rashi, Tosafos, Ritva, Beis Yosef, and the rest) are reached through the host text's location rather than being separately mapped to every mitzvah.
No cloudEverything — the mitzvah list, your reading position, your notes — lives in a local SQLite database with its own backup workflow.

Reader

Text the way it's actually learned.

Seforim aren't flattened into one scrollable file — each one keeps its own real structure, so a location you open today is the same location you reopen next month.

  • Bavli opens as daf/amud pages, the way the Gemara is actually cited.
  • Rambam opens by perek, down to the individual halacha.
  • Tur opens by siman, not as one giant document.
  • The reading pane uses a dedicated warm parchment surface — the one deliberately warm element in an otherwise flat, fatigue-resistant dark UI.

Ha'aros

A note is never just text.

Every note lives at an address — either a specific mitzvah, or a free/inbox slot when it isn't tied to one — and a source citation is never an afterthought.

  • Type a quoted line followed by (source) — it's rewritten into an attributed block quote automatically.
  • Full markdown inside notes and even inside the quote itself — bold, italic, tables, task lists.
  • Lightweight #hashtag tagging, parsed straight out of the text.
  • A plain spaced-repetition review pass — one more reason notes stay worth writing.
